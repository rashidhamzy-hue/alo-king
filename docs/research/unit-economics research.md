# Break-even (orders/day)  
Assume one courier + part-time dispatcher cost ~ $20–25/day (estimate).  We compare models:
- **Flat-fee ($3/order):** Revenue $3 ⇒ need ≈9 orders/day (25/3) (estimate).  
- **10% commission on $15 avg:** Rev $1.5 ⇒ need ≈17 orders/day (25/1.5) (estimate).  
- **15% commission on $15 avg:** Rev $2.25 ⇒ need ≈11 orders/day (25/2.25) (estimate).  

*Thus ~10–20 orders/day per route are needed to break even (est.). Lower fee or higher AOV reduces orders needed.*

# Merchant Costs & Fees  
- **Toters (Lebanon):** ~25% commission (blog). Restaurants reportedly “price in” this rate.  
- **Other apps (Zomato-style):** Similar range (~20–30%), with small merchants often absorbing it (estimate).  
- **WhatsApp SaaS (digital menu):** Avora eShop (Lebanon) starts at $10/month, zero commission (verified).  
- **Acceptable pricing:** Small merchants likely prefer modest fixed fees or low % rather than 25% cuts (estimate). For example, $10–20/mo (no cut) or ≤10–15% commission.

# Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)  
- **QR-code flyers:** Very low printing cost (~$0.1 each) (estimate); actual scans depend on placement.  
- **Click-to-WhatsApp Ads (Facebook/Google):** ~$0.5–1 per click (verified for restaurant search CPC, using as proxy). Converting click→order implies higher $/order.  
- **Village Facebook groups:** Essentially free (organic post/share) (estimate), but rely on word-of-mouth.  

# Operational Risks  
- **Power outages:** Lebanon often has rolling power cuts; residents get only few hours/day power (verified 2021). Service should support offline ordering and driver phone charging (generators/battery packs).  
- **Cash on delivery:** Risk of theft/fraud with couriers carrying cash; also reconciliations and needing exact change. (Common in MENA e-commerce). Mitigation: drivers travel in pairs, insured bags, or encourage digital payments (low credit card use).  
- **Failed deliveries/refunds:** Wrong address/unavailability wastes time & fuel. Need policy for refunds or discounts, and train drivers on verification. (No data found; standard challenge.)  
- **Peak hours:** Expect lunch (12–2pm) and dinner (7–9pm) peaks for restaurants; morning/evening grocery peaks. Plan drivers’ shifts accordingly.  
- **Seasonality:** Shouf is a summer tourism area (cool mountain retreats). Summer likely sees higher demand; winter (rain/snow) may drop demand and slow deliveries. Plan for off-season slowdown.

# 30-Day Pilot Plan  
- **Merchants:** Enroll ~15–20 (mix of restaurants & grocery). E.g. 3–5 venues per village. Provide digital menu setup (USD/LBP prices) and train staff.  
- **Villages:** Start in Baakline, Deir al-Qamar, Beiteddine, Dahr el-Baidar, (and outskirts of Aley). These cover main Chouf hubs. Choose merchants with interest.  
- **Drivers:** Begin with 5–10 couriers (allocating ~1–2 per village depending on size). Use freelance riders (app or call basis).  
- **Metrics (Estimates):** Aim for ~3–5 orders/day per village by end of week 2, rising toward 10/day by end of month. (Total ~25–50 orders/day network). Use cash on delivery in USD (users ease).  
- **Success criteria:** If by day 30 we reach at least ~5 orders/day/village *and* positive feedback from ≥50% of merchants (i.e. they see new sales), continue. If volumes stay very low (<3/village/day) or costs far exceed revenue, reconsider.

## Recommendation  
Fuel is extremely expensive (≈2.6M LBP/L, ~29 USD/L), so maximize short routes (≈0.9 USD fuel cost for a 3 km run). Given low expected volumes (~0.1% daily adoption) and high per-trip cost, a lean model is critical. We recommend **low fixed fees** (e.g. ~$3 per delivery) rather than high commission: local merchants already bear ~25% on existing apps, so a flat fee covers courier costs more transparently. For example, a $3 fee implies break-even ~9 orders/day (est.) per driver (vs ~17/day at 10% commission). 

Merchants could be attracted by avoiding commission: using a WhatsApp menu SaaS (Avora: $10–$30/mo, no cut) keeps their margins. At signup, highlight cost savings versus Toters/Zomato. Target onboarding to established eateries/cafés with reliable cuisine and one or two small groceries (to test bulk orders). 

Marketing should leverage **WhatsApp and Facebook** heavily: share digital menus via village WhatsApp groups and flyers. Online ads are costly (∼$0.5–1/click), so focus on grassroots: QR-code posters at taverns/churches and posts in community FB groups (essentially free). 

Given seasonal swings, note that summer tourism may provide a boost (the Shouf Biosphere area draws hikers in warm months). Pilot in summer/early fall to capture demand. Expect winter slow-down.

In summary, proceed with a 30-day trial in a few Chouf towns, charging a modest per-delivery fee (or small monthly fee) to merchants. Use local freelance riders and simple WhatsApp orders. Monitor orders/day and merchant satisfaction closely. If each village can achieve ~5–10 orders/day (covering driver pay) with rising trends, expand cautiously. Otherwise, reassess the model.
