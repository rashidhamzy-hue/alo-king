# **Spatial Hierarchy, Topographical Stratification, and Settlement Gazetteer of Lebanon: Technical Architecture for Conversational Commerce**

## **The Administrative and Territorial Architecture of Lebanon**

Lebanon’s territorial organization is established across three formal tiers of deconcentrated state authority and decentralized local administration, codified primarily under Decree-Law No. 116 of June 12, 1959, Law No. 522 of July 16, 2003, and Law No. 51 of September 7, 2017\. The primary administrative tier consists of nine Governorates (*Muhafazat*, singular *Muhafaza*), which function as deconcentrated territorial arms of the central government headed by a Governor (*Muhafiz*) appointed by the Council of Ministers under the statutory supervision of the Ministry of Interior and Municipalities (MoIM). With the structural exceptions of Beirut, which functions as an integrated metropolitan governorate without secondary district partitions, and Akkar, which forms a coterminous single-district governorate, each governorate is subdivided into administrative Districts (*Aqdya*, singular *Qadaa* or *Caza*), establishing twenty-six districts nationwide. Districts are headed by a Sub-Prefect (*Qaimaqam*), who oversees administrative compliance and acts as the immediate interlocutor for local municipal bodies.  
At the decentralized municipal level, the territory encompasses between 1,029 and 1,050 active Municipalities (*Baladiyat*, singular *Baladiya*), led by locally elected municipal councils and mayors (*Rais al-Baladiya*). Because significant expanses of agricultural terrain, rural mountain communities, and unorganized hamlets lack independent municipal councils, the physical geography of the state is mapped into 1,623 Cadastral Zones (*Manatiq Iqariyya*) maintained by the Central Administration of Statistics (CAS) and the Directorate of Cadastral Affairs under the Ministry of Finance. In territories lacking municipal councils, direct executive administration and municipal-grade service management are exercised directly by the *Qaimaqam*. To pool fiscal, operational, and heavy logistical resources, over fifty-seven Municipal Unions (*Ittihad al-Baladiyat*) have been formally chartered across the twenty-six districts, creating multi-jurisdictional spatial clusters responsible for inter-municipal roads, waste management, and regional commercial initiatives.

Territorial planning and commercial routing must accommodate historical micro-regions and socio-geographical subdivisions within these districts. The Chouf District, for example, is divided between its densely populated coastal strip, the historical high mountain core (*Chouf el-Aala*), and the southwestern highland plateau of *Iklim el Kharroub*. The Metn District bifurcates between an industrial littoral plain and the middle-to-high mountain clusters of Northern Metn (*Al-Matn al-Aala*). Similarly, the northern districts of Akkar and Minieh-Dannieh feature pronounced structural divides between flat, agricultural coastal plains (*Sahel*) and steep, high-altitude mountain massifs (*Jurd*).

## **Topographical Stratification and Regional Typologies**

Lebanon’s geomorphology transitions abruptly across a narrow spatial footprint of 10,452 square kilometers, ascending from Mediterranean sea level to elevations exceeding 3,000 meters over a linear distance of less than 35 kilometers. This severe altitudinal profile produces microclimatic, hydrologic, and road-network variations that heavily influence intra-district transit speeds, delivery costs, and year-round accessibility.  
The western maritime slope rises sharply from a narrow littoral strip through deeply dissected valleys into the Mount Lebanon range, culminating at Qornet es-Sawda at 3,088 meters. To the east, the range drops steeply into the Beqaa graben, an intramontane plateau resting between 850 and 1,150 meters above sea level. Beyond the valley floor rise the semi-arid slopes of the Anti-Lebanon mountain range and Mount Hermon (*Jabal el-Sheikh*), reaching 2,814 meters along the eastern frontier. These altitudinal shifts divide the country into five primary operational zones.

Settlements situated along the western maritime slope experience Mediterranean maritime climatic patterns, while communities on the eastern escarpment or within the Beqaa graben occupy continental and semi-arid regimes. The settlement of Bqaa Kafra, situated between 1,600 and 1,850 meters above sea level in the Bcharre District, represents the highest continuously inhabited locality in Lebanon and the wider Levant.  
Because steep terrain inflates surface travel times, transit models cannot rely on planar Euclidean distance. The effective delivery duration T\_{\\text{route}} over Lebanese terrain must be calculated as a function of horizontal distance, altitudinal climb, and road tortuosity:  
T\_{\\text{route}} \= \\frac{D\_{\\text{road}}}{V\_{\\text{base}}} \\cdot \\left(1 \+ \\alpha \\cdot \\frac{\\Delta h}{D\_{\\text{road}}} \+ \\beta \\cdot \\tau\\right)  
In this model, V\_{\\text{base}} represents the baseline velocity on level grades, ranging from 45 km/h on rural mountain arterials to 25 km/h in dense urban centers. The term \\Delta h / D\_{\\text{road}} measures the average vertical gradient, while \\tau denotes the tortuosity ratio (D\_{\\text{road}} / D\_{\\text{euc}}). The vehicle-specific calibration coefficients \\alpha and \\beta reflect mechanical sensitivity to slope and turning resistance, typically calibrated to \\alpha \\approx 2.1 and \\beta \\approx 0.45 for standard 125cc commercial delivery motorbikes.

## **Spatial Distance Networks and Inter-Village Corridors**

The arterial road network of Lebanon consists of four longitudinal corridors intersected by trans-mountain passes that bridge the Mediterranean littoral with the interior. Transportation flows depend heavily on:

> * The Coastal Highway Spine (Route 51), linking Naqoura, Tyre, Sidon, Beirut, Jounieh, Jbeil, Batroun, and Tripoli to the northern border at Arida.  
> * The Beirut-Damascus International Highway (Route 30), crossing the Mount Lebanon crest at Dahr el-Baidar pass (1,550 m ASL) into Chtaura and Masnaa.  
> * The Longitudinal Beqaa Valley Spine (Routes 39 and 45), connecting Rashaya and Western Beqaa northward through Zahle, Baalbek, Labweh, and Hermel.  
> * Trans-Mountain Arterials, such as the Bikfaya–Zahle road, the Faraya–Kfardebian to Hadath Baalbek pass, and the high-altitude Cedars–Ainata pass connecting Bcharre to Northern Beqaa at 2,590 meters ASL, which closes annually between November and May due to heavy snow accumulation.

## **Technical Architecture for WhatsApp-Native Ordering Engines**

Deploying a conversational commerce application across Lebanon requires designing for the local addressing realities of the country. Standard alphanumeric postal codes developed by the national operator LibanPost (such as 4-digit or 8-digit building codes like 2038 3054\) are almost entirely absent from consumer consciousness, commercial directories, and daily trade.  
Delivery navigation in Lebanon relies on a descriptive landmark hierarchy. Addresses are communicated through relational descriptions spanning:

> * The Administrative Governorate and District.  
> * The Official Municipality or Village name.  
> * The Local Sector or Neighborhood (*Hayy*).  
> * A Prominent Physical Landmark, such as a municipal hall, bakery, pharmacy, church, mosque, or roundabout.  
> * A Building Name or Architectural Marker (e.g., *Bineyit al-Salam, Facing Al-Makhzen Supermarket*).  
> * Vertical Floor and Apartment specifications (*Tabiq*, *Chaqqa*).

Furthermore, customer text input in chat channels often blends Lebanese Levantine Arabic script, Franco-Arabic chat numerals (*Arabizi* using numbers such as 2 for Hamza, 3 for Ain, 7 for Ha), standard French, and English within a single order thread (e.g., *"3al mafra2 ba3d el baladiyeh, binait al-nour, 2nd floor"*).

CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS postgis;  
CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS postgis\_topology;

CREATE TABLE governorates (  
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,  
    code\_iso VARCHAR(10) UNIQUE NOT NULL,  
    name\_en VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,  
    name\_ar VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,  
    geom GEOMETRY(MultiPolygon, 4326\) NOT NULL  
);

CREATE TABLE districts (  
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,  
    governorate\_id INTEGER REFERENCES governorates(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT,  
    code\_cas VARCHAR(20) UNIQUE NOT NULL,  
    name\_en VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,  
    name\_ar VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,  
    geom GEOMETRY(MultiPolygon, 4326\) NOT NULL  
);

CREATE TABLE settlements (  
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,  
    district\_id INTEGER REFERENCES districts(id) ON DELETE RESTRICT,  
    cas\_code VARCHAR(50) UNIQUE,  
    name\_en VARCHAR(150) NOT NULL,  
    name\_ar VARCHAR(150) NOT NULL,  
    sub\_region VARCHAR(100),  
    zone\_type VARCHAR(50) CHECK (zone\_type IN ('Coastal Littoral', 'Coastal Plain', 'Lower Mountain', 'Middle Mountain', 'High Mountain', 'Inland Valley', 'Semi-Arid Basin')),  
    elevation\_m INTEGER NOT NULL,  
    is\_coastal BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT FALSE,  
    centroid GEOMETRY(Point, 4326\) NOT NULL,  
    boundary GEOMETRY(MultiPolygon, 4326),  
    created\_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT\_TIMESTAMP  
);

CREATE INDEX idx\_settlements\_centroid ON settlements USING GIST (centroid);  
CREATE INDEX idx\_settlements\_boundary ON settlements USING GIST (boundary);  
CREATE INDEX idx\_districts\_geom ON districts USING GIST (geom);

CREATE TABLE address\_landmarks (  
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,  
    settlement\_id INTEGER REFERENCES settlements(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,  
    landmark\_name\_en VARCHAR(200) NOT NULL,  
    landmark\_name\_ar VARCHAR(200) NOT NULL,  
    landmark\_category VARCHAR(50),  
    aliases TEXT\[\],  
    location GEOMETRY(Point, 4326\) NOT NULL,  
    created\_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT CURRENT\_TIMESTAMP  
);  
CREATE INDEX idx\_landmarks\_location ON address\_landmarks USING GIST (location);

When an incoming WhatsApp location payload arrives, the database runs a point-in-polygon verification query to identify the corresponding settlement, district, and nearest known landmark within 750 meters:  
SELECT   
    s.id AS settlement\_id,  
    s.name\_en AS village\_name\_en,  
    s.name\_ar AS village\_name\_ar,  
    d.name\_en AS district\_name,  
    g.name\_en AS governorate\_name,  
    s.elevation\_m,  
    s.zone\_type,  
    ST\_Distance(  
        s.centroid::geography,   
        ST\_SetSRID(ST\_MakePoint(35.618452, 33.981245), 4326)::geography  
    ) / 1000.0 AS dist\_to\_center\_km,  
    (  
        SELECT al.landmark\_name\_ar   
        FROM address\_landmarks al  
        WHERE ST\_DWithin(al.location::geography, ST\_SetSRID(ST\_MakePoint(35.618452, 33.981245), 4326)::geography, 750.0)  
        ORDER BY ST\_Distance(al.location::geography, ST\_SetSRID(ST\_MakePoint(35.618452, 33.981245), 4326)::geography) ASC  
        LIMIT 1  
    ) AS nearest\_known\_landmark  
FROM settlements s  
JOIN districts d ON s.district\_id \= d.id  
JOIN governorates g ON d.governorate\_id \= g.id  
WHERE ST\_Contains(s.boundary, ST\_SetSRID(ST\_MakePoint(35.618452, 33.981245), 4326))  
   OR ST\_DWithin(s.centroid::geography, ST\_SetSRID(ST\_MakePoint(35.618452, 33.981245), 4326)::geography, 2500.0)  
ORDER BY ST\_Distance(s.centroid::geography, ST\_SetSRID(ST\_MakePoint(35.618452, 33.981245), 4326)::geography) ASC  
LIMIT 1;

## **Logistical Conclusions and Deployment Architecture**

Operating an on-demand delivery network across Lebanon requires configuring dispatch rules according to regional geography and infrastructure constraints. Fleet operational margins degrade if pricing relies solely on horizontal road distance. In coastal lowlands under 300 meters, couriers can operate lightweight 110cc to 125cc scooters at low operating costs. Once deliveries climb into middle and high mountain zones above 900 meters, fuel consumption increases substantially, mechanical wear accelerates, and transit speeds drop across steep switchbacks. Delivery pricing must therefore incorporate an altitudinal surcharge calibrated to total vertical climb.  
Dispatch routing must also account for seasonal weather disruptions. High-altitude settlements such as Faraya, Kfardebian, Tannourine, Ehden, and Bqaa Kafra experience regular snowfall and ice between November and April, periodically severing trans-mountain passes such as the Cedars–Ainata corridor and the Sannine ridge routes. The application's routing engine must maintain dynamic winter-closure tables, re-routing deliveries to local hubs or temporarily marking affected mountain destinations as inaccessible during severe storms.  
Finally, because conversational text in Lebanon often produces ambiguous landmark references (*Bineyit al-Baladiyeh* or *Al-Makhzen* can refer to hundreds of buildings across different towns), conversational ordering bots should always capture locations via native WhatsApp GPS coordinates first. Snapping coordinates against cadastral boundary polygons in PostGIS reliably establishes the administrative governorate, district, and village. Conversational text descriptions should then be reserved for last-mile details, such as the building name, floor number, and apartment intercom. This hybrid approach bridges Lebanon's informal landmark conventions with modern spatial routing engines.
